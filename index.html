01

Listen

A 4-microphone array on top of the cap detects speech and estimates which way it comes from, using its built-in voice processor.

02

Decide

An ESP32-S3 filters out jitter, waits out pauses between words, and ignores directions that jump around too much to trust.

03

Feel

Four small motors around the headband buzz toward the speaker. Halfway between two motors, both buzz together.

Built to be calm, clear and yours.

02

Every setting below lives on the cap itself, not on a server.

Any direction, smoothly
Blends neighbouring motors for in-between angles, or switch to "nearest motor" if that feels clearer.
Speech only
Reacts to voices detected by the mic's speech detector, not every bang and hum in the room.
Calibrated to you
Set which way is front, flip direction, and tune each motor's strength. Saved on the device.
One-touch pause
A single button pauses everything. Hold it to set front while someone talks ahead of you.
A transcript that knows the direction
Your phone's microphone hears the words, the cap knows the bearing. Each line is labelled with where it came from — or says plainly when the cap could not tell.
Standalone and private
No cloud, no audio recording. Close the app and the cap keeps working exactly the same.

What EchoSense is, and isn't.

03

Worth being straight about, because this is a prototype and people would be relying on it.

It does

  • Point you toward one active talker at a time, on a flat circle around your head.
  • Stop buzzing when people stop talking, or when you pause it.
  • Show an error pattern instead of guessing, if the microphone stops answering.

It doesn't

  • Tell distance, height, or who is speaking.
  • Reliably separate two people talking at once.
  • Replace a hearing aid, or warn about alarms and traffic. It is a research prototype.
